      The F-1 visa, step by step.

      After receiving an acceptance letter and an I-20 form from a US university, you pay the SEVIS fee, complete the DS-160 visa application online, and schedule a visa interview at the nearest US Embassy or Consulate. We provide comprehensive guidance for every step, including mock interviews.

      Get your I-20

      Your US university issues the Form I-20 once you are admitted and have shown how your studies will be funded. It is the document the rest of the process builds on.

      Pay the SEVIS I-901 fee

      The fee is paid online to the Department of Homeland Security before your interview. Keep the payment confirmation for your visa appointment.

      Complete the DS-160

      The online nonimmigrant visa application. You print the confirmation page and bring it to your interview.

      Attend your visa interview

      Interviews are generally required and are scheduled at the US Embassy or Consulate where you live. Our one-on-one mock interviews help you explain your study plans clearly.

      365 days
      Student visas for new students can be issued up to a year before your programme starts.
      30 days
      You can enter the US no earlier than 30 days before your programme start date.

      General information from the US Department of State and Study in the States (DHS). Rules, fees and timings change and every application is decided by a consular officer, so no outcome can be guaranteed. We will confirm the current requirements for your case.

      A potential pathway, not a promise.

      • Your degree must qualify under the DHS STEM Designated Degree Program List.
      • Your employment must meet the relevant OPT requirements, including being related to your field of study.
      • For the extension, your employer must participate in E-Verify and you need a completed Form I-983 training plan, along with other applicable requirements.
      • The extension must be applied for through the proper process and approved by USCIS before you can rely on it.
      • STEM OPT is temporary work authorization. It is not a job offer, a guaranteed visa extension or permanent residency.

      Eligibility and authorization are subject to applicable USCIS requirements and approval. Not all degrees or employment opportunities qualify.

      What are the basic requirements to study in the USA?

      Generally, you need a valid passport, strong academic transcripts, standardized test scores (like TOEFL/IELTS for English proficiency, and GRE/GMAT for graduates), letters of recommendation (LORs), and a compelling Statement of Purpose (SOP).

      When should I start my US application?

      Most US universities admit students for the Fall (August/September) and Spring (January) intakes, and many students start preparing 9–12 months ahead. Deadlines differ for every university and program, so we build a timeline around the ones on your shortlist.

      Do I need the GRE or GMAT?

      Many graduate programs ask for the GRE or GMAT, while a growing number are test-optional. Requirements vary by university and program, so we shortlist universities that match your current scores.

      Which English tests do US universities accept?

      Most US universities accept TOEFL or IELTS, and many also accept PTE or the Duolingo English Test. Minimum scores vary by university. We offer training, mock tests and free study material, and book your official test once you are ready.

      How do you help me choose a university?

      Our process experts evaluate your interests and previous academics, then suggest the most suitable STEM or non-STEM courses and colleges across America. We explain course syllabi, credit requirements and post-graduation scope so you can make the right decision.

      How does the F-1 student visa process work?

      After receiving an acceptance letter and an I-20 form from a US university, you pay the SEVIS fee, complete the DS-160 application online, and schedule a visa interview at the nearest US Embassy or Consulate. We guide you through every step, including mock interviews.

      How do you prepare me for the visa interview?

      We run one-on-one mock interview sessions and help you prepare strong financial and sponsor documents, so you can explain your study plans clearly and confidently to the consular officer.

      My visa was refused before. Can I apply again?

      A previous refusal does not stop you from reapplying. We review your earlier application, strengthen your documents and prepare you for the next interview. The final decision is always made by the US consular officer.

      How much does it cost to study in the USA?

      Tuition varies widely by university and program. As a guide, it often ranges from about $15,000 to $55,000+ a year, plus living costs. We help you compare options and plan your funding through loans, scholarships and on-campus work.

      Can I get financial aid or an education loan?

      Yes! We have tie-ups with reputed banks and financial institutions for both collateral and non-collateral education loans. These can cover tuition, accommodation and living expenses, and we connect you directly with bank executives.

      Are scholarships available for international students?

      Many US universities offer merit-based and need-based scholarships, assistantships and bursaries. We analyse your academic profile and guide you to apply where you have the best chance of funding.

      Can I work part-time while I study?

      Yes. On an F-1 visa you can usually work on campus for up to 20 hours a week while classes are in session, and more during official breaks. Off-campus work needs specific authorization, such as CPT or OPT.

      What is OPT and how long can I work after graduating?

      Optional Practical Training (OPT) may let eligible F-1 students work in the US for up to 12 months in a field related to their study. Graduates with a qualifying STEM degree may apply for a further 24-month STEM OPT extension — up to 36 months in total, subject to USCIS approval.

      What is CPT?

      Curricular Practical Training (CPT) lets eligible F-1 students take up paid or unpaid work, such as internships, that is an integral part of their course. It is authorized by your university's Designated School Official.

      Can I move from OPT to an H-1B work visa?

      Some graduates move from OPT to an H-1B visa. It needs an employer to sponsor you and is subject to an annual cap and selection process, so it is never guaranteed. We guide you on the pathway and what employers look for.

      Can my family come with me to the USA?

      Yes. Your spouse and unmarried children under 21 can accompany you on an F-2 dependent visa while you study. If you later move to an H-1B work visa, they can stay with you on an H-4 visa.

      Do you help with accommodation and pre-departure?

      Yes. We help you find safe, comfortable housing near your campus, and our pre-departure briefings cover US culture, academic expectations, packing and immigration procedures.

      What support do I get after I land in the USA?

      Your dedicated consultant helps with off-campus accommodation, getting your Social Security Number (SSN), finding authorized on-campus jobs, opening a US bank account and understanding medical insurance.

      Do I need health insurance?

      Most US universities require international students to have health insurance, either through the university's own plan or an approved alternative. We help you understand the options for your university.

      Why should I study in the USA?

      The United States is still the top destination for international students. It offers some of the world’s best universities, a flexible and diverse curriculum with a lot of hands-on, practical learning, and OPT/STEM OPT routes that may let you gain US work experience after you graduate.

      Which US cities are popular with international students?

      Popular choices include New York (Columbia, Cornell), Boston & Cambridge (MIT, Harvard), Washington, D.C., Chicago (University of Chicago) and the San Francisco Bay Area (Stanford, UC Berkeley, Silicon Valley). Every state has strong universities, so the best city depends on your course and budget.

      How many universities are there in the USA?

      Official US education data (IPEDS) lists around 3,969 degree-granting institutions across the country. You can search and filter all of them in our University Atlas.

      How does the US degree system work?

      A bachelor’s degree usually takes 4 years, and a master’s degree usually takes 1–2 years. PhDs typically take several more years. Courses are credit-based, so you can often mix core subjects with electives.

      Can I study in the USA after 12th grade?

      Yes. After Class 12 you can apply for a bachelor’s (undergraduate) degree. Universities look at your school grades and English test scores, and some ask for the SAT or ACT. We help you shortlist universities that fit your profile.

      What are STEM courses?

      STEM stands for Science, Technology, Engineering and Mathematics — for example computer science, data science, engineering and many science programs. A degree on the DHS STEM Designated Degree Program List may qualify you to apply for the STEM OPT extension.

      Do I need to show funds for the I-20 and visa?

      Yes. You need to show that you can pay at least your first year’s costs (tuition and living expenses) as listed on your I-20, using bank statements, sponsor documents, an education loan or scholarships. We help you prepare these financial documents.

      What is the weather like in the USA?

      It depends a lot on where you study. The Northeast and Midwest (New York, Boston, Chicago) have cold, snowy winters; California and the South are milder; the Southwest is hot and dry. We help you plan clothing and packing for your city.

      Is the USA safe for international students?

      Most university campuses have their own security, emergency services and international student offices. As in any country, safety varies by area, so we help you choose safe accommodation and share practical tips before you fly.

      Can I transfer to another US university?

      Often, yes. F-1 students can transfer their SEVIS record to another SEVP-certified school, working with the international offices at both universities. Credit transfer rules vary by university.
